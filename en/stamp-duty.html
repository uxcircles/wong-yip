What changed on 1 April 2025

The temporary thresholds from September 2022 ended. Stamp Duty now starts at £125,000 instead of £250,000, and first-time buyers pay nothing only up to £300,000 instead of £425,000. A £300,000 home that cost £2,500 in Stamp Duty under the old rates now costs £5,000.

Questions we are asked most

I sold my flat in Hong Kong before moving. Am I a first-time buyer?

No. First-time buyer relief is only for people who have never owned a home anywhere in the world. You pay the standard rates, but not the 5% higher rates, because this will be your only home.

I kept my flat in Hong Kong. Does that matter?

Yes. Homes you own outside the UK count, so buying in the UK means you own more than one and usually pay the 5% higher rates on top.

Can I get the 2% non-resident surcharge back?

Often, yes. If you spend at least 183 days in the UK in a continuous 365-day period that falls in the year before or after your purchase, you can claim it back from HMRC. Claims have time limits, so talk to us when you buy.

I paid the 5% higher rates because my old home had not sold yet. Can I claim it back?

Yes, if the new home replaces your main residence and you sell the old one within 36 months of buying the new one.

Who pays it, and when?

The return and payment are due within 14 days of completion. Your solicitor or conveyancer usually files it and adds the tax to their bill.

Letting it out from abroad

If you live outside the UK and let out a UK property, the rent is still taxable here. Without approval from HMRC, your letting agent or tenant must take basic-rate tax off the rent. We register you for the Non-Resident Landlord Scheme so you can receive rent in full, keep the rental accounts, claim allowable expenses and losses, and file your return each year.
